Math reference · domain/schedule/CareSchedule.kt

The adaptive watering model

Two independent knobs decide when a plant is next due for water: a confidence-weighted per-observation model that learns from what you tell it (#568, technical ADR-0021), and a computed seasonal curve that stretches or compresses the result by time of year (#569, product ADR-0026). This page is the mechanical reference for both, plus the cold-start and lifecycle-reset rules that keep the model honest. For the research that led to this design — the algorithm families considered and rejected — see watering-schedule-algorithms.html.

01

Three numbers, one plant

The model never touches the number the schedule actually uses directly — it touches a season-neutral base, and a separate function derives the day-to-day effective interval from it.

wateringBaseIntervalDays: Double?

Base

Season-neutral. The only number the adaptive model ever writes to. Stored unrounded — only the effective interval gets rounded to a whole day.

wateringIntervalDays: Int?

Effective

What CareSchedule.computeStatus() actually schedules against. = base when seasonal watering is off or the plant is pinned; otherwise base × season(today), rounded.

wateringConfidence: Int?

Confidence

null = never adapted. Otherwise 0–5: how much the model trusts its own base, and how cautiously it moves in response to new evidence.

02

The update rule

Every WATER log with adaptive watering on runs through CareSchedule.computeAdaptiveInterval(). One target, one exponential step toward it, two clamps.

target = observedGapDays × multiplier(feedback)
base′ = base + g(confidence) × (target − base)
base′ = clamp(base′, base × 0.60, base × 1.40) /* ±40% per step */
base′ = round(base′).coerceIn(1, 180)
A same-day duplicate (observedGapDays == 0) still clamps to at least 1 — a regression guard from #446, not a special case in the formula itself.

Confidence 0 (bootstrap / just reset) moves fastest; a dialed-in plant at confidence 5 barely budges on one new observation. currentConfidence == null (never adapted) uses the confidence-0 gain and reports back confidence 0, without evaluating a rise/fall transition.

03

How confidence itself moves

Confidence never rises just because a feedback chip was tapped — only from evidence that the schedule is (or isn't) predicting reality.

rises falls

Rises

Gap agreement — the observed gap lands within GAP_AGREEMENT_TOLERANCE (15%) of the current base: +1, capped at 5. Checked on every observation, feedback or not — a silent but on-schedule watering still counts as evidence.

Dialog dismissal — dismissing the ADR-0006 suggestion without applying says "the schedule is fine": +1, but capped at DISMISSAL_CONFIDENCE_CEILING (3) and never lowers an already-higher value. A habit-dismisser can't reach a level that implies real agreement.

Falls

Correction streak — correctionStreak() reads the most recent waterings' feedback (most-recent-first) and finds the signed run length of same-direction TOO_SOON/TOO_LATE calls. Two or more in a row: −2, floored at 0. JUST_RIGHT and null break a streak; a direction reversal stops it without counting the reversing entry.

Deliberately not cached on a column — logs can be edited or deleted, so the streak is recomputed from the last 3 waterings every time rather than trusted from a stored value that could go stale.

04

Off-schedule: ask why, the answer decides

Product ADR-0030 (#586), one rule for watering and rescheduling alike. "On schedule" is the exact same GAP_AGREEMENT_TOLERANCE test used above, surfaced as PlantCareStatus.isWateringOnSchedule — never a second notion of "close enough."

On scheduleobserved gap agrees with the effective interval — no prompt at all.
→
learns normally — full multiplier + gain for whatever feedback (usually null) was given.
Off schedule, attributedthe user is asked why and picks a reason that maps to TOO_SOON or TOO_LATE ("the plant needed it" / "it was dry by then").
→
learns normally — the explicit multiplier applies at full confidence-driven gain.
Off schedule, declinedasked why, picks "just my timing" / "I can't right now" — maps to null feedback on a gap that disagrees with base.
→
excluded — gain forced to 0. Logged as WATER_NOT_ATTRIBUTED. Confidence is not separately punished; it simply doesn't rise from this observation.

This third row is derived inside computeAdaptiveInterval() from timing (feedback == null && !gapAgrees(...)) rather than passed in as a boolean — a flag threaded through every quick-log surface, the AddCareLog form, a bulk log, and the notification's "Watered" action is one a caller can eventually forget. The four-state WateringFeedback column never grows a fifth persisted state to represent it.

05

The seasonal curve

domain/schedule/SeasonalWatering.kt — computed, not learned (ADR-0026: too little per-plant data to learn twelve monthly factors, and every houseplant broadly shares the same "faster growth in local summer" shape).

season(date, amplitude, hemisphere) = 1 + amplitude · cos(2π · (dayOfYear − peakDay) / 365)
effective = round(base × season(today)).coerceIn(1, 180)
peakDay = day 5 (northern hemisphere growth peak, early January); +182 for southern. Hemisphere is derived from the device's TimeZone ID — no location permission, no network.
SeasonalAmplitudeValueEffect at the summer peak
OFF0.00No seasonal adjustment — effective = base, always.
MILD0.20±20% swing across the year.
STANDARD0.35±35% swing (the registry default).
STRONG0.50±50% swing.

A plant can opt out entirely with pinIntervalToBase — effective interval then equals base year-round regardless of amplitude. Before an observed gap is fed into computeAdaptiveInterval(), it's de-seasonalized first (observedGap / season(dateOfGap)), so a plant that dried out fast in July doesn't get permanently marked thirstier once autumn's season factor drops back toward 1.0 — the model only ever learns about the season-neutral base.

06

Cold-start: bootstrapping from history

A plant with real watering history but no learned confidence yet (just turned the flag on, or just reset) doesn't have to start from scratch.

base = median over past waterings of (gapi / season(datei))
confidence = min(5, gapCount / 3)
Median, not mean — absorbs one outlier gap (a two-week holiday) without extra trimming logic. Applied only once MIN_BOOTSTRAP_GAPS (3 gaps → 4 waterings) is met; below that, a fresh flag-enable keeps the user's typed interval and a post-reset plant just stays at confidence 0.

Runs automatically on the next adaptive observation: once when wateringConfidence == null (first-ever observation — the whole history is eligible), or repeatedly while wateringResetAt is set after a lifecycle reset (eligible history bounded to what happened after the freeze/reset boundary — a gap not trusted for live learning isn't trusted for the bootstrap either). When it fires, the triggering observation's own suggested interval is discarded in favor of the bootstrap result, so the ADR-0006 dialog never re-surfaces a value the bootstrap already silently committed.

07

Lifecycle resets

Repot

Logging a REPOT care event resets wateringConfidence to 0 and sets a 28-day freeze (wateringFreezeUntil). Every observation inside that window is forced through the same exclusion path as an unattributed off-schedule watering — gain 0 — while confidence still updates normally: it's evidence about the schedule regardless of why base itself is being held back.

Room change

Moving a plant to a genuinely different room (any real change except blank → filled for the first time) also resets confidence to 0, but sets no freeze window — a room change is a smaller shock than uprooting the plant, and the model resumes learning immediately at the confidence-0 gain.

Both are written once, as a side effect at log-creation/plant-save time in domain/usecase/WateringLifecycleReset.kt — never derived live from querying REPOT history — so editing or deleting a past REPOT log can't spuriously re-trigger a reset later.

08

Worked example

A plant added with a typed interval of 7 days, adaptive watering on, no history yet (wateringConfidence = null). Three real waterings, three different outcomes — this is ADR-0030's thesis end to end.

A
Watered on day 9 (base was 7 → off schedule, gap ran long). Asked "Why was it late?", picks "It was dry by then" → TOO_LATE.

target = 9 × 0.82 = 7.38 · gain = g(0) = 0.60 (bootstrap, explicit feedback)
base′ = 7 + 0.60 × (7.38 − 7) = 7.70 → clamp[4.2, 9.8] → round → 9
base: 7 → 9
confidence: null → 0
excluded: no
B
9 days later, watered right on the new schedule (base = 9). No chip tapped — on-schedule, so no prompt appears at all → null feedback, but the gap agrees.

target = 9 × 1.00 = 9 · gain = min(g(0)=0.60, 0.15) = 0.15 (null-feedback cap)
base′ = 9 + 0.15 × (9 − 9) = 9 (nothing to correct) · gap agrees → confidence +1
base: 9 (held)
confidence: 0 → 1
excluded: no
C
13 days later (base = 9 → off schedule, gap ran long again). Asked why, this time picks "Forgot, or no time" — wait, that maps to TOO_LATE too; suppose instead they pick "Just my timing" → null, declining to attribute it to the plant.

null feedback + gap disagrees with base (13 vs 9, tolerance 1.35) → excluded, gain forced to 0.
base: 9 (held)
confidence: 1 (held)
excluded: yes — WATER_NOT_ATTRIBUTED
